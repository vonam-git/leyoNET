    Ports and services

    How ports distinguish programs on a device, which ranges exist according to RFC 6335 and which port numbers you should know.

    Address plus port

    The IP address brings a packet to the right device, the port to the right program. TCP and UDP each have 65,536 ports (16 bits). A connection is uniquely identified by five values: protocol, source IP, source port, destination IP, destination port. NAT (which replaces address and port) and the firewall (which filters by them) depend on this.

    Three ranges

    RFC 6335 divides the ports; IANA keeps the registry:

    RangeNameUse
    0–1023system ports (“well-known”)server services, on many systems only with administrator rights
    1024–49151user ports (“registered”)registered services of programs
    49152–65535dynamic ports (“ephemeral”)source ports of clients, new for every connection

    In leyoNET

    The packet list shows source and destination port for every TCP and UDP frame; the filter tcp.port == 80 shows only web traffic. Clients use source ports from 49152, servers reply from the well-known port. In the firewall and for port forwarding (DNAT), services are selected by their ports.

    Finding open ports: the network scanner

    On PCs, notebooks and servers the network scanner can be installed in the simulation under Programs (also as the command nmap). It finds devices by ARP and ping and sends a SYN to every port:

    ReplyState
    SYN/ACKopen: a service accepts connections
    RSTclosed: the device is there, but no service listens
    nonefiltered: usually a firewall drops the packet

    In real networks, scan only with permission.

    How to do it in leyoNET

    The same text as in the app’s help (F1 key).

    Network scanner

    The network scanner is a program for PC, notebook and server. To install it, open the desktop in the simulation, tab Programs, “Install”. Then there is the tab Network scanner and the command nmap in the Console of end devices.

    • Target: an address (192.168.1.20), a range (192.168.1.10-20) or a network (192.168.1.0/24, at most 256 addresses); IPv6 as an address, a range in the last group (2001:db8::1-20) or from /120
    • Search: In its own network every device shows up by ARP, in other networks by ping. The address map colours every address.
    • TCP ports: The scanner sends a TCP SYN. open = SYN/ACK, closed = RST, filtered = no reply (often a Firewall).
    • UDP ports (protocol “UDP”, nmap -sU): open = reply (e.g. DNS), closed = ICMP “port unreachable”, open or filtered = no reply (many UDP services do not answer an empty datagram).
    • TTL: From the TTL of the reply it estimates how many routers are in between.

    Everything shows up in the Packet list. In real networks, scan only with permission.

    Read on

    • TCP Reliable connections between programs: ports, three-way handshake, sequence and acknowledgement numbers, retransmission and teardown.
    • UDP The fast, connectionless transport for DNS, DHCP and RIP: no handshake, no acknowledgement, just ports and a checksum.
    • NAT and PAT Bringing private networks to the internet: port address translation (PAT), port forwarding, static SNAT and DNAT, 1:1 NAT and CGNAT at the provider.
    • Firewall rules (packet filter) Rules from top to bottom, default rule, stateful or stateless – how a router or firewall decides what may pass.
